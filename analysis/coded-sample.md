### Russell Group Cross-Cutting Observations

**The performative education problem.** Southampton, Glasgow, and Liverpool all open with education-framed language and then deliver regulatory instruments. The opening paragraphs and document bodies are written by different logics. Cardiff avoids this by saying almost nothing. KCL partially avoids it by creating an operational framework with genuine pedagogical structure. Durham avoids it most successfully by being explicit about what the document is and then going beyond that frame.

**What the computational ratio misses.** The ratio cannot distinguish between: (1) education language that does pedagogical work (Durham's ethical issues, KCL's four-level framework); (2) education language that does compliance work (Liverpool's "assessment is an important part of learning"); (3) education language that does aspiration work but no operational work (Southampton's "critically digitally literate"). The ratio is a useful first pass. The deficit model lives in the gap between what a document says it is doing (education) and what it is actually doing (regulation).

**The agency gradient.** Student agency runs from near-zero (Southampton: told what is permitted) to genuine (Durham: right to refuse AI entirely). The middle ground (Glasgow, Liverpool, KCL) delegates agency to module level, meaning students have whatever agency their individual tutors choose to grant.

**The trust architecture.** No policy explicitly states "we trust students." Durham comes closest by refusing to use the misconduct process for non-misconduct behaviour. The dominant model is conditional trust: granted in exchange for transparency.

**Summary: 5 genuinely educational, 4 performatively educational, 3 balanced, 8 detection-oriented.**

- **Education (5):** Stirling, Canterbury Christ Church, Arts University Plymouth, Durham, Edge Hill. These policies are structurally educational. Durham is reclassified from "balanced" because its pedagogical depth, explicit refusal to police "unwise" behaviour, and critical literacy resources make it functionally educational despite its misconduct framework location.
- **Performative (4):** Cardiff, Glasgow, Liverpool, Arts University Bournemouth. These policies use education vocabulary while structurally functioning as compliance or detection instruments. Glasgow and Liverpool are reclassified from "detection with veneer/aspirations" because their surface framing is educational even though their operational function is not.
- **Balanced (3):** King's College London, University of East Anglia, Edinburgh Napier University.
- **Detection (8):** Southampton, Goldsmiths, Loughborough, Coventry, Birmingham City, Winchester, Courtauld, Harper Adams.

### Key themes

1. **Performative education framing is the central finding.** The computational ratio cannot distinguish between education language that does pedagogical work and education language that does compliance work. The deficit model often lives in the gap between what a document says it is doing (education) and what it is actually doing (regulation).

2. **The ratio is accurate at the extremes, unreliable in the middle.** It correctly identifies policies above 0.5 as detection-oriented and policies below 0.05 as genuinely educational. Between 0.05 and 0.4, qualitative coding frequently disagrees with the computational classification.

3. **Where a policy sits institutionally matters more than what it says.** Policies located in academic misconduct frameworks (Goldsmiths, Loughborough, Winchester, Courtauld, Harper Adams) are overwhelmingly detection-oriented regardless of any educational language they contain.

4. **Student agency is the key differentiator.** The genuinely education-oriented policies (Durham, Stirling, CCCU, Arts Plymouth) share one feature: they give students genuine choices. The detection-oriented policies share the opposite: they tell students what to do.

5. **Module-level delegation is ubiquitous but ambiguous.** Nearly all policies delegate operational decisions to individual modules. This can mean genuine flexibility (Durham) or abdication (Coventry). The distinction depends on whether the policy provides a framework within which flexibility operates.

6. **No policy in the sample explicitly states "we trust students."** Durham comes closest by declining to use misconduct processes for non-misconduct behaviour. The dominant trust model is conditional: trust granted in exchange for transparency.

7. **Staff and student audiences receive different registers.** Where policies address both, staff typically receive pedagogical reasoning while students receive instructions (UEA is the clearest example).
